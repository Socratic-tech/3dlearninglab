MAKESPACE 1.0 · CLASSROOM GUIDE

Small changes.
Real design decisions.

A companion to visual CAD for middle-school students. Customize connected parts, predict the result, test, and improve.

The learning goal: Students use measurements and evidence to explain a design choice. The goal is not simply to move sliders or finish a print.

A first lesson in 45 minutes

  1. Notice · 5 minutes. Open A box that fits. Ask which dimensions should change together and which should stay fixed.
  2. Predict · 8 minutes. Measure a small object. Students record a goal and prediction in the Design notebook.
  3. Design · 15 minutes. Change one setting at a time. Record a trial, then make a revised version.
  4. Compare · 10 minutes. Partners explain their choices using dimensions and the 3D preview. If prints are available, compare physical results. Otherwise mark the trial as a digital check; do not treat it as a physical fit test.
  5. Explain · 7 minutes. Record the next change, download the notebook, and export a Project ZIP before leaving.

Printing typically happens between lessons. The fit-testing kit can supply shared classroom evidence for the box-and-lid activity.

Six starting points

FIT & MEASURE

A box that fits

Can you change the size of a box and still make its lid fit?

  1. Measure an object you want to protect. Give it a little extra room.
  2. Set the inside dimensions. Choose a lid clearance.
  3. Print the box and lid. Test the fit and record what you would change.

Evidence to look for

  • Your object fits inside.
  • The lid sits in the opening without force.
  • Wall thickness stays the same when the box changes size.

Prompt: Change the inside width. The outside walls and the lid update together. What stays the same?

TEST & IMPROVE

The fit detective

How much space does your printer need between two parts?

  1. Choose a pin diameter and a starting clearance.
  2. Print the three labeled holes and the matching pin. Change only one variable.
  3. Try the pin in each hole. Record the best fit for this printer and material.

Evidence to look for

  • You predict a result before testing.
  • You test all three holes with the same pin.
  • Your next design uses clearance supported by your results.

Prompt: The three holes use different clearances. Predict which will fit best before you print.

PATTERNS & LOGIC

Puzzle workshop

Can a drawing of squares become a 3D puzzle piece?

  1. Draw a small grid and mark the squares your piece will fill.
  2. Click the grid squares to draw your piece. Colored squares become solid blocks.
  3. Compare the drawing and model. Change one row and predict the new shape.

Evidence to look for

  • Your pattern matches your drawing.
  • Your piece is connected.
  • You can explain how the row pattern becomes a solid.

Prompt: Click squares to change the shape. Keep the colored squares touching along their edges.

IDENTITY & SCALE

Make your mark

How can a tag stay readable when the name or size changes?

  1. Choose a name or short message. Decide who will use the tag.
  2. Set the size and clip hole. Check readability from a distance.
  3. Print and test it with a clip or ring. Revise one setting.

Evidence to look for

  • All letters fit on the tag.
  • The clip hole stays inside the edge.
  • The message is readable at the finished size.

Prompt: Try a short name and a long name. The lettering resizes to stay inside the tag.

BALANCE & SUPPORT

Stand & deliver

How does a stand support an object without tipping?

  1. Measure the width and thickness of the object your stand will hold.
  2. Choose the base depth, back height, and angle. Predict where it could tip.
  3. Test on a level surface. Record what changes make it more stable.

Evidence to look for

  • The device sits behind the front lip.
  • The base stays flat on the table.
  • Your design changes respond to an observed test.

Prompt: Measure your device and its case. Compare a deep base with a shallow one before printing.

SPACE & EFFICIENCY

A place for everything

Can you organize the same space in more than one useful way?

  1. Choose the items you want to organize. Measure the biggest one.
  2. Set the footprint and divide it into useful spaces.
  3. Compare two layouts. Which uses the space better, and why?

Evidence to look for

  • Your objects fit into the compartments.
  • The dividers stay evenly spaced.
  • You can justify the number and size of compartments.

Prompt: Change the number of compartments. The dividers resize and space themselves evenly.

What the file types keep

  • STL: the current shape, in millimeters. Choose all parts or one part above the preview. Preview colors are not saved as printing colors.
  • Project ZIP: editable code, selected settings, teacher labels, supporting files, and notebook. This is the best backup and classroom handoff.
  • SCAD: editable OpenSCAD code with the chosen settings and portable lettering outlines. Other dependencies need the Project ZIP.
  • Notebook: a readable Markdown document for collecting predictions, settings, results, and reflection.

Alongside Tinkercad and SimpCAD X

Use MakeSpace to create functional parts whose dimensions follow rules. Download an STL, then use Import in the visual editor to add it to a larger design. Return to the MakeSpace Project ZIP when changing the original fit or relationships. No file is sent to another service automatically.

Prepare your own student project

Open a .scad file or ZIP, then use Teacher tools. Simple top-level numbers, text, booleans, and numeric vectors become controls. Computed expressions stay in code. Labels and visibility can be customized under Student controls.

// Give the setting a useful description
width = 55; // [35:1:90]

/* [Details] */
shape = "round"; // [round:Round,square:Square]

/* [Hidden] */
$fn = 48;

Ranges follow [minimum:step:maximum]. Put one setting per line. PuzzleCAD 2.2 loads automatically. Other supporting files can be added individually or as a ZIP; folder paths are kept. Lettering uses Abel outlines, so font choices in imported projects are substituted. Complex scripts can exceed browser memory or the 60-second build limit.

Saving, access, and classroom setup

Projects save in this browser on this device, including supporting files. They do not automatically follow a student to another browser or computer. Export a Project ZIP at the end of a session. Clearing browser data can remove saved work. The project shelf supports copies, renaming, and recoverable archiving.

There are no accounts, student rosters, or cloud submissions. Teacher tools is not password protected. Your school's normal process should handle collecting student work. After the offline indicator appears, this site can run from the browser's cached app files, subject to browser storage availability.

Before printing

The preview is not a strength or printability certificate. Check the STL in your slicer, choose suitable orientation and supports, and use your classroom's normal printer supervision. Fit depends on printer, material, and settings; the clearance values are experiments, not guarantees. The volume shown is solid geometry volume, not an estimate of filament used with infill.
